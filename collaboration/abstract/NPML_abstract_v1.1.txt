Cryogenic detectors targeting eV-scale energy thresholds operate in regimes where physical signals are deeply embedded in correlated noise, posing challenges for conventional reconstruction methods. In the DELight experiment, superfluid helium detectors instrumented with magnetic microcalorimeters aim to detect sub-GeV dark matter and low-energy neutrino interactions through faint phonon and quasiparticle signals.

We formulate signal reconstruction in this regime as a noise-aware representation learning problem. Starting from the classical optimal filter formalism, we generalize reconstruction using expectation-maximization principal component analysis (EMPCA), which learns detector signal subspaces directly under the noise covariance metric. This approach enables data-driven extraction of signal structure in low signal-to-noise environments.

Using simulated detector traces with realistic correlated noise, we compare optimal filtering with EMPCA-based reconstruction and demonstrate improved robustness and energy resolution for faint signals. We further show that EMPCA can be interpreted as a noise-weighted linear autoencoder, providing a conceptual bridge between classical matched filtering and modern representation learning.

Finally, we discuss extensions toward nonlinear autoencoders for event classification and simulation-based inference techniques such as normalizing flows for likelihood-free parameter estimation. These results highlight how modern AI/ML methods can enhance reconstruction pipelines in next-generation rare-event detectors relevant for neutrino and dark-matter searches.
