Privacy Policy – Scientific Computing Service

Information pursuant to Article 13 GDPR for the use of an academic scientific computing and storage service operated by Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT).

This privacy policy applies to the processing of personal data in connection with the operation and use of an academic scientific computing service (the “Service”), including authentication via federated identity (EGI AAI), user account management, job scheduling (e.g., HTCondor), and technical operation/security logging.

The Service is intended for scientific research and collaboration among KIT and participating academic institutions.

2. Purposes of Processing and Legal Bases

The Service processes personal data for the following purposes:

  • Authentication and authorization to grant access to the Service and its resources.
  • Operation of user accounts (provisioning, role/group assignment, deprovisioning).
  • Technical operation of compute and storage services, including scheduling and execution of jobs.
  • Resource accounting and capacity planning (e.g., CPU time, memory usage, storage usage), including grant- and project-related reporting obligations.
  • IT security, misuse prevention, troubleshooting, and incident handling.

Legal bases (depending on the specific processing activity) include: performance of a task carried out in the public interest (Art. 6(1)(e) GDPR) and, where applicable, legitimate interests in maintaining secure and reliable IT operations (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). Applicable German state and institutional regulations (e.g., higher education and KIT legal framework) may apply in conjunction.

3. Categories of Personal Data Processed

3.1 Federated Identity Data (EGI AAI)

When you authenticate via EGI AAI, KIT receives a subset of identity attributes released by the federation and/or your home institution. These may include:

  • Name
  • Email address
  • Affiliation (institution)
  • Federated unique identifier (e.g., persistent subject identifier)
  • Group/VO membership attributes relevant for authorization

3.2 Local Account Data

  • Service username / local account identifier
  • SSH public keys (where used)
  • Authorization metadata (roles, groups, project/VO allocations)
  • Contact information necessary for account administration (typically email)

3.3 Resource Usage and Accounting Data

For fair resource allocation, stability, and reporting, the Service processes technical usage data such as:

  • Job submission metadata (timestamps, job IDs, queue/project association)
  • CPU usage (e.g., CPU time)
  • RAM usage
  • Runtime duration
  • Storage usage (quotas, consumption)
  • Service-level accounting metadata (e.g., allocation units for projects/VOs)

The Service does not use resource-usage data for profiling, automated decision-making, or evaluation of individuals beyond what is necessary for operation, fairness, and security (e.g., detecting abuse or misconfiguration).

3.4 Security and System Logs

To ensure secure and reliable operations, the Service processes security and system logs, which may include:

  • Login events (timestamps, source IP address)
  • Failed authentication attempts
  • Administrative and service health events relevant to incident response
  • Scheduler-related metadata required for troubleshooting and integrity (e.g., HTCondor events)

4. Integration with Research Infrastructure

4.1 HTCondor and GridKa

The Service may use HTCondor to schedule and execute jobs, and jobs may be submitted to or executed on connected research infrastructure (e.g., GridKa) depending on project configuration and authorization.

Where jobs are executed on external infrastructure, relevant job/accounting metadata may be exchanged as required for technical operation and accounting. External infrastructure operators may maintain their own logs and accounting records under their own responsibility.

4.2 CVMFS

The Service may use CVMFS (CernVM File System) for read-only software distribution. CVMFS is used to deliver software stacks and environments. Research data is not stored in CVMFS by the Service.

5. Research Data and User Responsibility

The Service is intended for scientific research data. Users are responsible for ensuring that uploaded and processed content complies with applicable laws and policies. In particular, the Service is not intended for storing or processing special categories of personal data (Art. 9 GDPR) unless explicitly authorized and governed by an appropriate legal basis and safeguards.

KIT does not routinely inspect or evaluate research content. Processing of research content is limited to technical storage, access, and computation requested by users.

6. Recipients of Personal Data

Personal data may be accessed by or disclosed to:

  • Authorized KIT personnel responsible for operating and securing the Service (e.g., system administration, IT security).
  • Federated authentication infrastructure (EGI AAI) as part of the authentication flow (as applicable).
  • Connected research infrastructure operators (e.g., GridKa) where job execution requires exchange of operational and accounting metadata.

Personal data is not sold and is not disclosed to commercial third parties for marketing purposes.

8. Security of Processing

KIT applies appropriate technical and organizational measures to protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of the Service, including (as applicable):

  • Encrypted transmission (e.g., SSH/TLS) for administrative and user access
  • Role-based access control and least-privilege administration
  • Firewalling, network segmentation, and system hardening
  • Monitoring for security-relevant events and abuse prevention
  • Log retention limited to what is necessary

9. Your Rights

Under GDPR, you have rights regarding your personal data, including:

  • Right of access (Art. 15 GDPR)
  • Right to rectification (Art. 16 GDPR)
  • Right to erasure (Art. 17 GDPR), where applicable
  • Right to restriction of processing (Art. 18 GDPR)
  • Right to data portability (Art. 20 GDPR), where applicable
  • Right to object (Art. 21 GDPR), where applicable

If processing is based on consent in a specific context, you may withdraw consent at any time with effect for the future (Art. 7(3) GDPR).
